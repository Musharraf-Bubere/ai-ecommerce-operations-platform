# Problem Discovery Research Note 03

## Existing Third-Party E-commerce Operations Solutions

**Project:** AI E-commerce Operations Platform
**Research stage:** Competitor and solution research
**Status:** Preliminary desk research; product gap not validated
**Last updated:** 2026-10-29

---

## 1. Purpose

Investigate existing third-party software that helps e-commerce businesses manage orders, shipping, returns, inventory, and operational workflows.

The objective is to understand existing capabilities before deciding whether our project should address a specific unmet need.

This research uses publicly available product documentation. Vendor descriptions establish what a company advertises, not independent proof of performance or customer outcomes.

## 2. Product A — Veeqo

**Website:** https://www.veeqo.com/ecommerce-order-management-software

**Official Amazon product information:**
https://sell.amazon.com/tools/veeqo

### Documented capabilities

Veeqo describes capabilities including:

- Centralized order management across multiple sales channels.
- Shipping and bulk label purchasing.
- Inventory management and stock synchronization.
- Order filtering, tagging, and prioritization.
- Shipping automation rules.
- Multichannel reporting and operational insights.
- Return and refund management.

### Relevance to our research

Veeqo overlaps with the proposed idea of collecting order information and helping sellers manage fulfilment operations.

A basic order dashboard, shipping workflow, or routine order-status automation would not be a sufficiently differentiated product direction by itself.

### Remaining questions

- Which features and integrations are available to sellers operating in India?
- Which order and fulfilment exceptions does Veeqo identify automatically?
- Does it help prepare evidence-based marketplace support cases?
- What tasks remain manual after a seller adopts it?

### Evidence limitation

The capabilities above are based on Veeqo's published product information. They do not establish that every feature is available in every country or seller account.

## 3. Product B — AfterShip Returns

**Website:** https://www.aftership.com/returns

**Official help documentation:**
https://support.aftership.com/en/returns

### Documented capabilities

AfterShip Returns describes capabilities including:

- Return and exchange workflows.
- Return eligibility and routing rules.
- Return shipment tracking.
- Return labels and notifications.
- Return analytics.
- Integrations with supported e-commerce platforms and logistics systems.
- APIs and webhooks for supported integrations.
- AI-powered exchange recommendations on certain plans.

### Relevance to our research

Returns management, return tracking, policy automation, and return analytics are already established software categories.

Building a generic returns management application would therefore require a clear, evidence-backed reason to choose it over existing products.

### Remaining questions

- Which integrations are suitable for our intended Indian seller segment?
- Does the product support the marketplace-specific return workflows we are investigating?
- Does it help sellers investigate discrepancies between shipment records and physical receipt?
- What evidence gathering or support-case preparation remains manual?

### Evidence limitation

The published capabilities do not establish that AfterShip addresses every marketplace-specific seller problem. Regional availability and specific integrations must be verified.

## 4. Product C — Linnworks

**Website:** https://www.linnworks.com/

**Official feature documentation:**
https://www.linnworks.com/features/

### Documented capabilities

Linnworks describes capabilities including:

- Multichannel order and inventory management.

### Relevance to our research

Linnworks demonstrates that centralized operations, cross-channel visibility, and workflow automation are established areas of e-commerce software.

The proposed project must not assume that these capabilities are absent from existing platforms.

### Remaining questions

- Which exception-investigation workflows does the platform support?
- Which integrations and features are available to the target seller segment?
- Does it help assemble evidence across separate systems into a single support case?
- Which tasks still require manual investigation?

### Evidence limitation

The product's published descriptions do not independently establish how well it handles the specific scenarios in our initial research.

## 6. Preliminary Research Finding

Existing third-party products already cover substantial parts of e-commerce order management, shipping, returns, inventory, and automation.

Consequently, a generic order dashboard, returns tracker, or AI-powered operations assistant is not yet a justified product direction.

A narrower hypothesis worth investigating is whether sellers experience a costly manual workflow when investigating operational exceptions and assembling supporting evidence across systems.

This remains an unvalidated hypothesis.

## 7. Questions for Further Investigation

1. Which operational exceptions are not adequately handled by existing products?
2. Do sellers manually collect evidence from multiple systems when opening or following up on support cases?
3. How frequently does this happen?
4. How much time, money, or operational effort does the process consume?
5. Which existing tools do sellers already use?
6. Are the relevant products and integrations accessible to sellers in India?
7. Can the required data be accessed through permitted, reliable interfaces?
8. Would sellers adopt a separate tool rather than use their existing systems?
9. What measurable outcome would demonstrate that a new tool is useful?

## 8. Provisional Decision

**Decision:** Do not commit to building a generic order-management, returns-management, or shipping application.

**Status:** Provisional research direction.

**Reason:** Existing products already advertise substantial functionality in these categories.

**Trade-off:** A narrower product may serve a smaller initial audience, but it provides a better opportunity to validate a specific need.

**Revisit when:** Additional evidence and seller feedback identify a specific unmet need that existing products do not adequately address.

## 9. Research Limitations

- This is desk research based primarily on vendor-published documentation.
- Product descriptions may emphasize strengths and omit limitations.
- No independent hands-on product testing has been completed.
- No seller interviews have been conducted.
- Regional availability and specific integrations require further verification.
- No market-size, cost-saving, or performance claims have been independently established.
- No product gap, architecture, or feature scope has been finalized.

## 10. Next Action

Investigate real seller workflows and reported operational problems.

For each potential problem, record:

- Original source and date, where available.
- The specific task the seller is trying to complete.
- The current tools and workaround.
- Explicitly reported time, cost, or operational impact.
- Existing products that may address the problem.
- Evidence supporting or contradicting the proposed opportunity.
- Remaining questions requiring seller interviews or product testing.

Do not begin implementation until a specific problem is supported by sufficient evidence.
