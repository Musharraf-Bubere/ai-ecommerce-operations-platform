
# Seller Workarounds and Manual Effort Research

## 1. Purpose

This research investigates the manual work sellers perform when handling two operational problems:

- Candidate A: A shipment is physically handed over, but the marketplace does not record the expected pickup scan.
- Candidate B: A return is marked delivered or completed, but the seller reports that the product was not physically received.

The objective is to identify:

1. What sellers currently do.
2. Which manual activities are involved.
3. What information and evidence they collect.
4. Where repeated follow-up occurs.
5. Whether existing marketplace tools adequately support the workflow.
6. Whether an independent application could reduce meaningful operational effort.

This document distinguishes directly reported seller activities from assumptions about common seller practices.

---

## 2. Research Method

The research uses publicly accessible sources:

- Marketplace seller discussions.
- Marketplace moderator responses.
- Official support workflows.
- Seller-reported investigation experiences.

Evidence is classified as:

- Official platform guidance.
- Individual seller report.
- Seller-reported workaround.
- Research interpretation.
- Unknown or unverified information.

Public forum reports are not representative samples.

The number of reports found must not be interpreted as the frequency of these problems across the seller population.

## 3. Candidate A — Missing Pickup Scan

### Problem

A seller physically hands a shipment to a logistics partner, but the marketplace continues to show the shipment as awaiting pickup.

Potential consequences include:

- Delayed shipment status updates.
- Late-shipment metrics.
- Order cancellation.
- Seller performance concerns.
- Disputes about whether pickup actually occurred.

### Evidence 1 — Amazon pickup without scanning

Source:

https://sellercentral.amazon.in/seller-forums/discussions/t/beff1b23-955a-45c4-be83-84eab93bf2c6

A seller reported that an Amazon Easy Ship pickup executive collected a parcel but did not scan it.

The seller reported that:

- The marketplace did not record the pickup.
- The order was automatically cancelled.
- The customer received a refund.
- The seller contacted Seller Support.
- The seller requested an investigation of pickup scan logs.

Amazon's moderator response explained that an unscanned pickup could be treated as a missed pickup and directed the seller to the Pickup Miss Form when the applicable waiting period had passed.

Another seller replied that they had experienced the same issue.

#### Manual Activities Identified

- Checking shipment status.
- Contacting Seller Support.
- Requesting investigation of pickup records.
- Submitting the Pickup Miss Form.
- Monitoring the case and shipment outcome.

### Evidence 2 — Pickup scanning delays

Source:

https://sellercentral.amazon.in/seller-forums/discussions/t/ac3040f9-0a8c-4255-b52b-8ad22eb5e601

A seller reported that parcels were collected but not scanned on the pickup associate's device.

The seller described:

- Delayed pickup status updates.
- Parcels remaining in Waiting for Pickup.
- Late-shipment metrics.
- An order cancellation.
- A cancellation fee.
- Contact with the pickup associate.
- Previous communication with Seller Support.
- Submission through the Easy Ship Pickup Miss panel.

The seller also reported being given repeated explanations about a system problem.

#### Manual Activities Identified

- Calling or contacting the pickup associate.
- Rechecking marketplace statuses.
- Submitting pickup-miss information.
- Contacting Seller Support.
- Following up when the issue remained unresolved.

### Evidence 3 — Official Amazon workflow

Source:

https://sellercentral.amazon.in/seller-forums/discussions/t/cc316f3b-10c3-4fbc-a2eb-29fd87e5a95e

Amazon describes a process for handling missed pickups:

1. Contact the pickup associate using the available contact details.
2. Check the pickup status in Manage Orders.
3. Submit the Pickup Misses Issue Form when the specified conditions are met.
4. Provide affected order IDs.
5. Wait for investigation and pickup rescheduling.
6. Create a support case if the problem continues.

This confirms that sellers already have an official process.

The application should complement this workflow rather than duplicate the form.

### Candidate A — Manual Work Summary

Directly evidenced activities:

- Contacting pickup associates.
- Checking order and shipment statuses.
- Submitting marketplace forms.
- Providing affected order IDs.
- Contacting support.
- Requesting investigation of scan records.
- Monitoring corrections to shipment status and seller metrics.

### Candidate A — Unverified Activities

The research has not established how frequently sellers use:

- Excel or Google Sheets.
- Dedicated handover registers.
- WhatsApp groups for pickup investigations.
- Custom seller-built automation.

These practices should not be presented as established market behavior.

## 4. Candidate B — Return Marked Delivered but Not Received

### Problem

A marketplace records a return as delivered or completed, but the seller reports that the returned product was not physically received.

Potential consequences include:

- Product loss.
- Reimbursement disputes.
- Repeated support interactions.
- Evidence collection.
- Claim rejection and appeal.
- Time spent following up on unresolved cases.

### Evidence 1 — Multiple returns not received

Source:

https://sellercentral.amazon.in/seller-forums/discussions/t/ea7e417f-bcb8-4d4e-b453-f02d417f15c2

A seller reported that five customer returns had not been physically handed over, despite more than 500 units having been shipped.

The seller described:

- Repeated support requests.
- Difficulty obtaining a resolution.
- Being asked to provide CCTV footage from months earlier.
- Concern about the lack of delivery proof.

Amazon's moderator recommended filing individual SAFE-T claims for the affected returns and sharing support case IDs for follow-up.

#### Manual Activities Identified

- Identifying affected return orders.
- Checking which returns were physically received.
- Locating potentially relevant CCTV footage.
- Raising separate SAFE-T claims.
- Retaining support case references.
- Following up on unresolved cases.

Important:

This is one seller's report. It does not establish that five missing returns out of 500+ shipments is a representative incident rate.

### Evidence 2 — Repeated SAFE-T appeals

Source:

https://sellercentral.amazon.in/seller-forums/discussions/t/07e0030e-cba7-475b-9947-e636d8e949c0

A seller reported that a return was marked as attempted or delivered even though the seller stated that no delivery attempt occurred.

The seller described:

- A rejected SAFE-T claim.
- Repeated appeals.
- Requests for delivery-attempt evidence.
- Requests for timestamps, call records, and attempt scans.
- Continued financial loss.

The seller reported a loss of ₹6,199 for the affected order.

Another seller replied with a report of substantially higher claimed losses.

These amounts are individual seller claims, not independently verified financial measurements.

#### Manual Activities Identified

- Checking return delivery status.
- Raising a SAFE-T claim.
- Reviewing claim rejection.
- Preparing an appeal.
- Requesting delivery-attempt evidence.
- Following up with support.

### Evidence 3 — CCTV and delivery records

Source:

https://sellercentral.amazon.in/seller-forums/discussions/t/5fc587af-d432-49a0-b188-588c5f08f1d2

The discussion describes sellers being advised to retain supporting evidence such as:

- CCTV footage.
- Delivery logs.
- Communication records.

It highlights the importance of retaining records that may be needed during a later dispute.

### Candidate B — Manual Work Summary

Directly evidenced activities:

- Checking physical receipt against marketplace status.
- Identifying disputed returns.
- Searching for CCTV footage.
- Collecting delivery-related records.
- Requesting proof of delivery or attempted delivery.
- Raising individual claims.
- Preparing appeals.
- Tracking support case IDs.
- Following up on unresolved claims.

### Candidate B — Unverified Activities

The research has not established how frequently sellers use:

- Excel or Google Sheets for return disputes.
- Separate claim-tracking workbooks.
- WhatsApp groups for return investigations.
- Custom evidence-management systems.
- Third-party applications specifically for missing-return claims.

These remain questions for further validation.

## 6. Existing Workarounds and Tools

### A. Marketplace Forms

Amazon provides a Pickup Miss Form and Seller Support workflow.

These already handle the official reporting process for missed pickups.

A new application should not simply recreate the form.

### B. Phone Calls

Seller discussions describe contacting pickup associates to clarify pickup status or obtain an explanation.

This is a manual communication channel.

The research has not established whether sellers consistently maintain records of these conversations.

### C. CCTV and Physical Records

Some return-dispute reports describe requests for CCTV footage and other supporting records.

This creates a possible evidence-retention challenge.

The exact retention practices of individual sellers remain unknown.

### D. Support Cases and Claims

Both problems can involve:

- Marketplace forms.
- Support cases.
- Individual claim references.
- Additional evidence requests.
- Follow-up.
- Escalation.

The application could potentially organize these activities, but existing marketplace systems already provide the official submission and resolution channels.

### E. Spreadsheets and WhatsApp

The current research did not find sufficient direct evidence that sellers commonly use Excel, Google Sheets, or WhatsApp specifically to manage these two investigation workflows.

General e-commerce articles describe spreadsheet and messaging-based order management, but that is not sufficient evidence for this narrower use case.

We must not claim that spreadsheets or WhatsApp are established workarounds for these disputes without stronger evidence.

## 7. Potential Software Improvements

### Candidate A

Potential application responsibilities:

1. Maintain a shipment handover register.
2. Record seller-reported handover time.
3. Associate manifests or receipts with shipments.
4. Compare seller-reported handover records with marketplace events.
5. Identify missing or delayed events.
6. Maintain an investigation timeline.
7. Record pickup-miss form submissions.
8. Track support case references.
9. Record status corrections and outcomes.

The application must distinguish seller-reported handover from independently verified carrier pickup.

### Candidate B

Potential application responsibilities:

1. Maintain a return investigation register.
2. Identify returns marked delivered but not received.
3. Record seller receiving confirmation.
4. Track proof-of-delivery requests.
5. Associate received POD documents.
6. Associate CCTV or receiving-register references.
7. Track claim submission dates.
8. Track appeal and follow-up deadlines.
9. Maintain the final investigation outcome.

The application must distinguish requested delivery proof from received and verified proof.

## 8. Potential AI Responsibilities

AI may help with:

- Summarizing investigation histories.
- Extracting order references from documents.
- Extracting timestamps for human review.
- Organizing evidence.
- Drafting support-case explanations.
- Summarizing support responses.
- Highlighting missing information.

Deterministic application logic should handle:

- Order and shipment matching.
- Timestamp comparison.
- Duplicate detection.
- Missing event detection.
- Deadline calculation.
- Case status tracking.
- Evidence association.

The seller must review and approve claims and support communications.

AI must never invent evidence or convert an unverified statement into a confirmed event.

## 9. What the Research Establishes

### Established by Available Evidence

1. Sellers have publicly reported pickup collection without scanning.
2. Sellers have reported delays between physical pickup and marketplace status updates.
3. Sellers have reported returns marked delivered without physical receipt.
4. Some sellers describe contacting associates and support.
5. Some sellers describe filing individual claims and appeals.
6. CCTV footage, delivery logs, and communication records can become relevant to return disputes.
7. Marketplace support workflows already exist for both problems.

### Not Established

1. How frequently these problems occur across sellers.
2. Average investigation time.
3. Average financial impact.
4. The percentage of cases successfully resolved.
5. How many sellers use spreadsheets for these investigations.
6. How many sellers use WhatsApp as their investigation record.
7. Whether existing third-party applications already manage these workflows adequately.
8. Whether sellers would adopt an independent application.
9. Whether sellers would pay for the proposed functionality.

## 10. Comparative Interpretation

### Candidate A — Missing Pickup Scan

Potential strengths:

- A relatively clear operational trigger.
- A manageable shipment-level investigation.
- An identifiable marketplace reporting process.
- Potential for proactive event reconciliation.
- Potential to maintain a handover record.

Potential weaknesses:

- Marketplace pickup-miss workflows already exist.
- Independent physical handover confirmation may be difficult.
- A seller register may create additional work if not integrated with existing operations.
- The frequency and financial impact are unknown.

### Candidate B — Return Not Received

Potential strengths:

- Potentially significant product-value losses.
- Clear claim and support workflows.
- Evidence collection and deadline tracking may be important.
- Multiple records may need to be organized.

Potential weaknesses:

- Delivery evidence may require external cooperation.
- Claim decisions depend on marketplace policies.
- Evidence requirements vary.
- Existing SAFE-T and support workflows already exist.
- The frequency and financial impact are unknown.

Neither candidate has been selected.

---

## 11. Product Validation Conclusion

The research identifies repeated manual activities in publicly reported seller cases.

Candidate A involves:

- Pickup coordination.
- Status checking.
- Missing-scan reporting.
- Support follow-up.

Candidate B involves:

- Return receipt verification.
- Delivery evidence collection.
- Claim preparation.
- Appeals and follow-up.

Both workflows offer possible opportunities for better information organization and investigation management.

However, the research does not establish that an independent application would be sufficiently valuable to sellers.

The strongest unresolved questions are:

- How often do these cases occur?
- How much time does each investigation consume?
- What financial consequences result?
- Which workarounds do sellers actually use?
- Are existing tools insufficient?
- Would sellers use and pay for a dedicated solution?

Further broad documentation research is unlikely to answer all these questions.

Direct seller validation or access to anonymized real investigation records would provide stronger evidence.

---

## 12. Recommended Next Step

Stop broad research and move to a product-selection decision supported by the available evidence.

Before implementation, seek at least some direct seller validation where possible.

If direct seller access is unavailable, a prototype may be built only as a clearly labeled technical hypothesis, using synthetic or anonymized test data.

Do not claim market validation based on public forum reports alone.

## Research Integrity

This document records preliminary findings from public seller reports and marketplace responses.

Seller reports are individual accounts and have not been independently verified.

The research does not claim:

- That the problems are frequent across the seller population.
- That reported losses are representative.
- That every seller performs the same manual activities.
- That spreadsheets or WhatsApp are common investigation tools.
- That existing marketplace workflows are inadequate.
- That the proposed product has validated demand.
- That sellers would pay for the proposed solution.

Direct seller validation remains necessary.
